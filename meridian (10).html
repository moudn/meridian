(06) The House — A Note From the Founder (LDN)

(06.1) — WHY MERIDIAN EXISTS

I grew up between places — and I noticed that what I remembered of each one was never the view. It was the smell of rain arriving, of incense in a stairwell, of citrus peeled by someone I love.

Those scents were never ours to invent. Long before perfume was an industry, it was a craft held in particular hands: the attar distillers of Kannauj who have coaxed the monsoon out of baked earth for four hundred years, the incense makers of the Kathmandu valley, the pickers of Hangzhou who know the exact hour the osmanthus gives itself up. They are the original perfumers of these places. MERIDIAN begins as their student.

And they are disappearing. Globalisation flattens what it touches, and the algorithm glamourises what is already expensive — the same ten houses, the same lifestyles, crowned again every morning. A craft with no follower count, held in a village without signal, by hands that have never been photographed, is treated as though it doesn't exist. So the workshops empty a little more each year, the children of distillers leave for the cities, and a scent that took four centuries to perfect can be gone in one generation of being ignored. We may be the last generation that gets to choose otherwise.

So every fragrance in Folklore is researched where it was born — learned slowly, as guests. We work with the artisans who pioneered these accords rather than around them: their materials, bought at the price the craft deserves; their methods, kept intact where industry would flatten them; their names, printed on our labels. A share of every bottle returns to the workshops that taught us.

That is what this house is for. Not to take a culture's scent and translate it, but to keep the original speaking — so the craft survives, the makers are known, and the world gets to smell the way it actually is.
